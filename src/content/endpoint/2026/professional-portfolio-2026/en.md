## The point of comparison

In 2024, I built my professional portfolio in the traditional way. I designed its structure, wrote the code, prepared the styles, organised the content, and manually solved the development problems. The work took approximately twenty hours.

It served its purpose, but it was a basic product with a limited structure, less visual depth, less content, and an architecture with modest ambitions.

In July 2026, I needed a portfolio that better represented my experience, my current profile, and the way I work. This time, the initial active work took thirteen hours.

But the 2026 portfolio is not simply a faster reproduction of the previous one: it is a product on a different scale.

## Two products that are difficult to compare

The previous portfolio was essentially a static personal presentation. The new one includes:

• a defined visual identity; • complete Spanish, English, and French versions; • navigation adapted to each language; • light and dark themes; • responsive design and keyboard accessibility; • semantic structure;

• professional experience, education, and distinctions; • a technical blog called Endpoint; • independent article pages and article navigation; • a carousel-based projects section;

• SEO, social metadata, structured data, and a sitemap; • automated publication through GitHub Actions; • a custom domain and visitor analytics; • technical repository documentation.

The difference is not twenty hours versus thirteen to make the same thing. It is twenty hours to manually build a basic portfolio versus thirteen to direct, implement, review, and publish a much more complete product.

## Productivity did not increase only in speed

When people discuss productivity with AI, they often imagine that a task that once took ten hours now takes five. That measurement is incomplete.

In my experience, the most important change was at the boundary of what was reasonable to attempt. Without AI, every feature carried a considerable cost. Adding a language also meant maintaining parallel structures, reviewing links, adapting metadata, and checking behaviour. Creating articles required an architecture, templates, routes, and consistency reviews. Accessibility, dark mode, SEO, a carousel, or structured data each added hours and opportunities for error.

When every detail costs too much, scope is reduced to manage time and energy. AI changed that equation: features that would previously have been discarded could be incorporated, tested, and corrected quickly.

The improvement was therefore not merely time saved, but an expansion of the product that became possible.

## My role did not disappear: it changed

During the construction of the 2026 portfolio, I wrote no code manually. This does not mean that I asked for “a portfolio” and received a finished product.

My work consisted of defining the objective and professional identity; selecting what the site needed to communicate; providing references; evaluating alternatives; setting priorities; approving copy; detecting inconsistencies; correcting visual problems; reviewing results; and configuring publication.

ChatGPT participated in analysis, writing, and requirements organisation. Codex inspected the repository, implemented changes, and ran technical validations. The process was AI-augmented, but it was not autonomous.

AI could produce an implementation, but it could not decide which version best represented my identity, which tone was appropriate, which information was exaggerated, or which visual composition failed. Every iteration depended on human evaluation.

## The ability to correct became more important than the ability to write

In a traditional process, much of the energy is spent translating decisions into code. When that translation accelerates, the centre of the work shifts.

The questions become: is the problem well defined? Does the solution represent what I need? Is the architecture appropriate? Does the experience work across devices? Did AI introduce incorrect assumptions? Was something changed that needed to be preserved?

Writing code stops being the only constraint. Judgement, review, and the clarity needed to describe what must be built become more valuable.

AI can quickly generate a plausible answer, but it can also generate errors and mediocre decisions. Productivity appears when the developer distinguishes between a functional solution and one that deserves to be accepted.

## The cost of iteration fell dramatically

Many adjustments emerged during development: sections that were too long, cards outside the viewport, misaligned titles, hierarchy problems, language changes that returned to the home section, differences between versions, pending links, disproportionate logos, and editorial corrections.

In manual development, each revision requires locating files, modifying code, checking regressions, and repeating validations.

With Codex, a correction could be formulated as a precise set of conditions. The tool inspected the implementation, made the change, and ran technical checks. This did not eliminate review; it made review more frequent.

Because correction was less costly, I could demand more from the result. Instead of accepting the first functional version, I continued refining it. That is another form of productivity: being able to reject, at lower cost, what has not yet reached the desired standard.

## It was not a controlled experiment

The comparison has limits. I did not build a current version entirely by hand in parallel, nor did I estimate how many hours each feature would have required without AI. I also have more experience today and a clearer idea of the intended outcome.

It would therefore be irresponsible to claim that AI made development exactly five, eight, or ten times faster. What can be stated is:

• the previous portfolio required about twenty hours of manual work; • the new one reached its first published version in thirteen hours; • the new product has considerably greater complexity and quality; • no code was written manually; • direction, review, and acceptance remained human.

The meaningful evidence is not a universal acceleration figure, but the observable difference between the two outcomes.

## Quality is also productivity

Productivity is often measured by counting tasks, lines of code, or time saved. But producing more does not always mean producing better.

The gain appeared in visual coherence, professional presentation, additional languages, accessibility, better architecture and documentation, automated publication, search visibility, and room for evolution.

AI made it possible to devote less effort to repetitive execution and more to the central question: what product do I want to build, and what is still missing for it to represent me correctly?

## The developer as director of the process

The developer does not stop needing technical knowledge; it must be applied at another level. The developer needs to understand the code being accepted, recognise errors, evaluate architecture, formulate requirements, anticipate consequences, and verify that the solution answers the real problem.

Someone without technical judgement can generate a great deal of code with AI, but also a fragile or unmaintainable application. The advantage appears when the tool amplifies existing capabilities: systems understanding, experience, architectural judgement, analysis, attention to detail, business knowledge, and validation discipline.

AI did not replace my responsibility for the product. It multiplied the speed at which I could convert decisions into verifiable implementations.

## An initial conclusion

I expected to save time, not for the main effect to be a higher achievable outcome.

In 2024, twenty hours of manual work produced a basic portfolio. In 2026, thirteen hours of human-directed, AI-augmented work produced a multilingual, extensible, accessible, measurable professional platform prepared to grow.

The difference is not only that I arrived sooner. It is that I went further.
